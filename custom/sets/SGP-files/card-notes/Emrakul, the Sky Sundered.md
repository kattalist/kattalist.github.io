## The Storm That Lasted Forever
### A *Stirrings in Goldport* Story

The Hall of Dominion resisted. Marble pillars, wrought in the ancient style, bore agonized wear from the latter days. Uprooted trees had whipped across the Hall’s facade and left their brand across the entire front row of columns, before being shorn from their mooring and cast into the sea. Lantha’s pilgrimage was only possible in this quaint spot now, where the wind had dulled to such a point that branches but not trunks were flying through the sky. There was scarce sun, but no skylights either, just a purple-grey fog, a mass of distant appendages that ravaged the rest of the world and left storms in their wake.\
	Frossenhold had been gone for months. What about Kaalham? Ivenglade? The Guard was two days behind on its routine check-in. Perhaps the telegraph lines had fallen, or perhaps they had. The great being writhing behind the city was so colossal that it was hard to make out distances. It could be that Goldport was the only place left in existence.\
	The great doors gave little resistance in the gale. From all his months advocating for the Institute, Lantha had swift recall of the place. Past the unmanned security checkpoint, up a flight, and across to the throne room. The wind roared low through the brick, into a cavernous rumble that shook the foundation and swayed the chandeliers. His journal burned in his satchel. His revolver burned in his pocket.\
	He knocked once, more like a thud to see if the door would give, then kicked until it broke from the hinges. Gold-trimmed chairs had been set for a vacant audience. Minister Karsten trapped Lantha in his molten gaze, readying his cane. The Duchess scarcely moved, head bowed. Behind her, the immense windows revealed the last specks of unobstructed sunlight and the grey, foamy roil below. There was a third figure Lantha did not recognize; they were hunched and gaunt, and the knuckles of their mummified hand clutched the nape of Zaila’s long, white veil. \
“You need to leave immediately.” Karsten charged across the room, unscrewing the tip of his cane with such fluidity as to be surely practiced. A bayonet, glistening and jagged, poked from the shaft.\
	“You need to listen to me.” Lantha stepped back to the hallway. He dug his hand into his pocket, felt the cold metal trigger. “The Institute figured it out. We know what’s been going on. We know you kept this from all of us.”\
	“If they had known, the city would be in flames by now.”\
	“It’s the same thing.” Glass splintered on the floor above, perhaps a window that a stray rock or brick had blown out. It let in the gale, which ravaged the corridors with a hideous howl. “If they had only known what was coming, they could have faced it. You’ve taken their agency.”\
	“There was never any agency.” His smile was something new now. Amorphous, then alien. Latent with saccharine cunning. “Torvinald is dead. The Guard is dead. You travelled here; you saw the scene outside. How could they not be? How could anyone avert the apocalypse?”\
	Lantha reached for his journal, rifling for the page he had expected to call upon. The results of the Astrion expedition. The hedrons. “The same way we did last time. Thousands of years ago, we had no cars, no automata, no airships. None of the comforts of today, and when we were under siege, we built these. In precise alignments, they can bind or repel. We cast them away, and then forgot them.\
	“We uncovered a hedron in Frossenhold, in the catacombs. Three more in Astrion. More in the southern swamps, or the northern mountains. We could have learned from them. We could have built them ourselves. And yet, when we brought this before you, you laughed us off. You called us paranoid. You said our job was to investigate murders, not to meddle in matters of historical record. And now, we’re dead. Because you killed us all.”\
	The wind made for the building’s only sound. Karsten looked down to his cane, which he clutched tightly like a club. It drooped again to his side. Zaila and the other figure stood unmoving back through the doorway.\
	“I… did.” Karsten’s breaths were heavy. “I trusted Her Majesty. She reassured us and told me to tell the public the same. Maybe the signs were there all along. Maybe ambition blinded me. I just don’t know anymore.” He dropped his cane, which clattered against the marble. Another moment passed. His expression hardened and he reached down again. “She did this. She did this.”\
	He turned, charged the pair, readied, swung. A single scream - his - became a wrenching wail. From where the blade impacted Zaila’s shoulder sprouted tentacles, enclosing impossible geometry. It was as though the cane, then the arm, then the torso were folding upon itself, kneading and creasing through higher dimensions. He turned to Lantha, who had scarcely ever seen greater terror in a man’s eyes. Begging him to draw the gun. To take the shot. To end whatever endless agony he had embarked upon. The shriek had a tenor and bass now; it echoed as a thousand dissonant voices, before fading with him alongside him into the ground and back into the Duchess’ body. Something writhed and contorted under her veil. Her gaunt accomplice stared at Lantha with two empty sockets and beckoned with a single finger.\
	He took each step towards the throne assuming it was his last. Each echoed up and through the ceiling ornaments. Hundreds of years of history had been etched into this place. A lineage that stretched unbroken to the Old Ages. It bled into the fine woodwork of the throne, which bore the crest of House Eculen. Four generations past, and now he stood at the feet of the fifth. “Your Majesty.”\
	*Lantha Desaerin. Scholar. Detective. Foolish vigilante of the truth.* Raspy, coarse whispers plumbed his mind.\
	“You knew all along.”\
	*I did. The traitor Clanston tried, but never understood.*\
	“He funded the Institute, which funded the Task Force. Stelver figured it out. What did you do to him?”\
	*He and Clanston dug too deep. They died believing in answers.*\
	“Answers to what?”\
	*Why. Why the world is ending. Why the Eye opened. Why people betray.*\
	“Are those not answerable questions?” Quietude had washed over him. Assimilation and obliteration were now the same in his mind.\
	*The answers are unsatisfactory.*\
	“I don’t care. I want to hear them.”\
	She gave the slightest head turn, over to the accomplice. They nodded.\
	*The world is ending because of me. I opened the Eye. People betray for the promise of a better reward.*\
	“Why did you betray?”\
	*Power. It was promised by Kavalesk’s divine word. Another head turn. And then it was granted.*\
	“You let it in.”\
	*Them. I let them in.*\
	Lantha’s heart sank. “What are they?”\
	*Titans from beyond this world. Reshapers of all. They are here to cleanse this place. To make it pure in their image.*\
	“I don’t understand. You had power.”\
	*You could not hope to understand this power. Their gifts have remade me. I have become their equal.*\
	“And if this world dies, you die too.”\
	*I and I alone can leave. There are more places out there, unlike this one. More worlds to transform and to conquer.*\
	For the first time, Lantha addressed Kavalesk. “But you…”\
	*They shall join me. Their devotion shall be rewarded with the ultimate form of eternal existence.* From beneath her shawl extended a pruned lavender hand. They reached out for it.
	Kavalesk laughed haughtily. More of a cough at first, as though they had to relieve their voice of the burdens of age. It became a grunt, then a struggle as they befell the fate of Karsten. Their sockets burned with the fire of life, all they could muster. Their mouth hung open, and out fell a blistering screech. Tentacles choked the throat before the entire face faded into empty mass, like the destruction of a clay sculpture.\
“RUN” was all Lantha made out before he sprinted away. As he shot down the stairs and out through the foyer against the compulsion of wind, the last screech of Kavalesk and Zaila’s grim cackle haunted him all the way.\









*Dearest reader,*\

*This is the final message I shall ever write. Minutes ago, warning bells engulfed the city. The enemy is at our walls, and cannot be stopped. The fight is over. We have lost. This is written for the benefit of any who shall come across it, perhaps in a better time where power does not corrupt and where evil has been vanquished from all worlds.*\

*As you read this, you may look out over endless seas of ruin, monolithic husks of brick and steel. I ask you to remember Goldport for what it was. The birthplace of the steelsoul. The idyllic dream of our time. The jewel Calloran spun around. Within the walls which have undoubtedly fallen, two million were born, lived, and died. They had dreams beyond anything I could summarize. I ask that you remember them as well.*\

*Remember Marsellus Clanston and his son Leonard, both of whom led the Institute. In a tireless pursuit of knowledge, they lifted so many of us. They discovered the first signs of the incursion, and commissioned the Task Force to fix it. With mortal danger awaiting us, three hundred scientists and investigators pieced together the truth. We uncovered Astrion. We reverse-engineered the hedron. We could have saved everyone. The Clanstons deserve to be remembered as though they did.*\

*Remember Lex Torvinald. The preeminent steelsoul, military champion, defender of us all. He led the Golden Guard to certain demise without fear, in the hopes that we would find the time to save everyone else. He inspired human and automaton alike.* \

*Remember Karsten. His propaganda campaign deceived us all, and yet in his final moments, he attempted to do what was right. There is no longer any point in remembering anything but the good.*\

*Remember Arn Stelver. Though much record of him is lost, his findings are summarized here. He led the most perilous expeditions we embarked upon. He kept me sane. He was my truest friend through it all.*\

*Remember his wife Emeria and his sons, Brythe and Liano. They died in service of the noblest of causes, and surely rejoice with him now in whatever comes next.*\

*Remember my wife Conora. She was beautiful as the night sky, shining with all the splendour of a billion stars. She was the smartest, kindest, gentlest woman I ever knew. She was the good in all of us, crafted by angels, sculpted by the rising sun. She deserved none of this. None of us did.*\
